SUAS 2025
— first place, globally.

An autonomous fixed-wing platform for the Student UAS competition in Maryland. 30 teammates, 81 international teams, and — after two years of iteration — the top spot on the leaderboard.

The competition

The Student Unmanned Aerial Systems (SUAS) Competition, hosted annually in Maryland, USA, challenges university teams to design, build, and operate an autonomous UAV capable of executing complex surveillance, search-and-rescue, and payload-delivery missions. It is judged on technical design, flight readiness, and live mission performance.

Role

As Analysis & Technical Lead on Team Arrow (Nirma University's 30-member UAV team), I was responsible for the aerodynamic and structural validation of the vehicle, coordination across sub-teams, and the team's technical reporting to the SUAS technical review board.

What I owned

  • Aerodynamic analysis: airfoil selection, wing planform iteration, stability & control studies across the mission envelope — using XFLR5 (panel method) and ANSYS Fluent (RANS) in tandem.
  • Structural evaluation: load cases and FEA verification of wing spars, fuselage bulkheads, and payload mounts in ANSYS Mechanical.
  • Mission planning & autonomy: Mission Planner / ArduPilot flight-stack configuration, waypoint planning, geofence setup, and ground-station integration.
  • Technical reporting: led preparation of the design report and technical briefs submitted to SUAS.
  • Mentorship: onboarded and trained junior members on CFD/FEA pipelines — establishing team simulation standards.

Result

1st place overall among 81 international teams at SUAS 2025. The flight systems executed the mission end-to-end autonomously, and Team Arrow's technical report placed among the top submissions.
