PINN Airfoil Self-Noise
— architecture search & epoch sensitivity.

Uniform and cascading physics-informed neural networks for trailing-edge noise prediction on the NASA NACA 0012 dataset — 54 configurations across three experiments, R²=0.981 at 5,000 epochs.

Context

Airfoil self-noise — specifically turbulent boundary-layer trailing-edge (TBL–TE) noise — is the dominant acoustic emission from wind turbines, aircraft fans, and propeller-driven UAVs at low Mach numbers. Classical semi-empirical models such as Brooks–Pope–Marcolini (BPM) are fast but narrow in applicability; high-fidelity computational aeroacoustics is accurate but prohibitively expensive. This project uses physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) as a middle ground: a feedforward network regularised by a physical smoothness constraint derived from Ffowcs Williams–Hall acoustic theory.

Conducted as a term project for the Minor in Data Science at Nirma University, co-authored with Dharmik Jha and Vanshi Raiththa. Three interconnected experiments were carried out to systematically evaluate architecture and training-budget effects.

Dataset and Feature Engineering

The NASA NACA 0012 self-noise dataset contains 1,503 wind tunnel measurements from NASA Langley Research Center. Each sample records a unique combination of frequency, angle of attack, chord length, free-stream velocity, and suction-side displacement thickness. The prediction target is the Scaled Sound Pressure Level (SSPL, 103.4–141.0 dB). Three features were engineered from acoustic theory to bring the input dimension to nin = 8:

  • Chord Reynolds number Re = U∞c/ν — captures the combined velocity–chord effect (ν = 1.5 × 10−5 m²/s).
  • Strouhal number St = fc/U∞ — the natural non-dimensional frequency in trailing-edge noise scaling.
  • Log-frequency ln(f) — linearises the two-decade frequency range; validated by LES studies of NACA 0012 beyond the BPM experimental envelope.

Split: 80/20 (1,202 train / 301 test, random_state=42). Both inputs and SSPL were independently z-score normalised using training-set statistics only. Target normalisation proved the most critical implementation detail: without it, the data loss operates at O(100) dB² while the physics loss operates at O(0.01–0.1), creating a scale mismatch that effectively disables the physics term regardless of epoch count.

Physics Loss

No explicit PDE governs the empirical SSPL relationship, so the physics penalty encodes a known physical property: the aeroacoustic response surface is smooth and continuous in all input parameters. Large predicted gradients are physically implausible. The combined loss is:

Ltotal = (1/N) Σ(ŷ − y)² + λ · ⟨‖∂SSPL/∂x‖²⟩

The gradient penalty is evaluated via TensorFlow's nested GradientTape automatic differentiation. Weighting λ = 0.1 and Adam at η = 10−3 were held constant across all experiments. All training used the full 1,202-sample training set as a single batch per epoch.

Architecture Variants

Uniform-width PINN: D hidden layers each containing W neurons (ReLU), one linear output. Parameter count Nparams = ninW + DW² + W + 1.

Cascading-width PINN: Layer width halves geometrically at each depth — Wi = ⌊W₀/2i⌋ for i = 0,…,D−1. For D=4, W₀=256 this produces the sequence 256–128–64–32 (87,041 parameters). Early wide layers extract rich feature representations; narrower final layers act as a learned dimensionality reduction before the scalar output. This architecture concentrates representational capacity where it matters most, consistent with the theoretical result that depth increases linear regions exponentially while width increases them only polynomially.

Experiment 1 — Uniform Grid (1,500 epochs)

18 configurations crossing D ∈ {2,3,4,5,6,8} and W ∈ {64,128,256}. All achieve R² > 0.95. Width 256 consistently outperforms narrower configurations at equal depth; optimal depth at W=256 is D=5, with D=6 and D=8 nearly identical (R²=0.9743), indicating that additional depth beyond 5 has saturated the network's representational gains. Experiment 2 — Cascading Grid (1,500 epochs)

Same depth × starting-width grid evaluated with the cascading architecture. Best result: D=4, W₀=256 (256-128-64-32) achieves R²=0.9784, surpassing the best uniform result (R²=0.9749) by ΔR²=0.0035 while using ~87K parameters versus ~295K — 3.4× more parameter-efficient. Two systematic failure patterns emerged: complete failure at D=8 for W₀ ∈ {64,128} where geometric halving reduces the final layer to a single neuron (a degenerate rank-1 projection), and performance degradation at D≥5 for all W₀ where cascading removes capacity faster than additional depth can recover it.

Experiment 3 — Epoch Sensitivity

The best uniform configuration (D=5, W=256) was re-trained from scratch at five epoch budgets: 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000, and 5,000. Performance improves monotonically across the full range. The steepest gain occurs between 500 and 1,500 epochs; improvement rate slows but does not saturate at 5,000 epochs, where the model reaches R²=0.9810 — exceeding the Gradient Boosting benchmark (R²=0.966) by ΔR²=0.015.

Outcomes and Future Work

  • Cascading architectures achieve equal or better accuracy than uniform networks at equal depth with 3.4× fewer parameters — practically valuable in latency-constrained deployment such as real-time blade design optimisation.
  • Target normalisation is the critical training stability factor: it resolves the loss scale mismatch between the physics and data terms that caused earlier 300-epoch results (best R²=0.939) to underperform.
  • Replacing the generic gradient-smoothness physics loss with explicit BPM aeroacoustic residuals (U∞5 velocity scaling, Strouhal-number frequency law) would encode harder physical constraints.
  • A cosine learning rate schedule would improve convergence efficiency beyond 2,000 epochs; current results show diminishing returns at constant η = 10−3.
  • Dropout regularisation on the best cascade configuration and skip-connected (residual) architectures for very deep configurations remain as open experiments.
