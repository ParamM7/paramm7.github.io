interEHDFoam
— energy-coupled multiphase EHD solver.

Extending a volume-of-fluid electrohydrodynamic solver to couple sensible-enthalpy transport — enabling thermally-driven simulations of Taylor bubble dynamics in capillary channels under external electric fields.

Background

Multiphase electrohydrodynamics (EHD) studies the coupling between fluid motion, a deformable interface, and externally applied electric fields. The OpenFOAM solver interEHDFoam — an EHD variant of interFoam — resolves two-phase flow via the volume-of-fluid (VoF) method and augments the momentum equation with a Maxwell-stress body force computed from the solved electric potential field. It is well-suited for capillary-scale problems where surface tension, viscous forces, and electrical body forces interact across a sharp interface.

In the base solver, thermal effects are absent: fluid properties (conductivity, permittivity, surface tension) are treated as constant. This is a significant limitation because many practical EHD applications — electrohydrodynamic heat exchangers, two-phase cooling loops, electrically enhanced boiling — involve strong temperature gradients that feed back into the electrical and interfacial problem. The temperature dependence of electrical conductivity alone can cause order-of-magnitude variation across an interface in thermally stratified flows.

Objective

Extend interEHDFoam at the solver level to couple the sensible-enthalpy transport equation — enabling simulations in which the temperature field modulates local electrical conductivity, permittivity, and surface tension, and these modifications in turn feed back into the electric body force, the interface dynamics, and the velocity field. The extension must preserve full compatibility with OpenFOAM's PIMPLE loop, native dictionary-based case setup, and the upstream VoF/EHD infrastructure.

Governing Equations

The coupled system solved at each PIMPLE outer iteration consists of five field equations:

  • Continuity: ∇·U = 0
  • Momentum: ρ[∂U/∂t + (U·∇)U] = −∇p + ∇·(μ∇U) + fσ + fEHD
  • VoF phase fraction: ∂α/∂t + ∇·(αU) + ∇·[α(1−α)Ur] = 0
  • Electric potential: ∇·(εE) = ρe ;   E = −∇φ
  • Sensible-enthalpy transport (new):
ρ ∂h/∂t + ρ(U·∇)h  =  ∇·(k∇T) + ∇·(Me·U) + Φvisc

where h is the sensible enthalpy, k the thermal conductivity (phase-averaged), Me = ε(E⊗E − ½|E|²I) the Maxwell stress tensor, and Φvisc the viscous dissipation rate. The EHD body force in the momentum equation is fEHD = ρeE − ½|E|²∇ε, combining the free-charge (Coulomb) and dielectrophoretic (gradient-permittivity) contributions.

Solver Extension — What Was Modified

Energy transport equation

Finite-volume discretisation of the sensible-enthalpy equation added to the solver source files. Convective flux assembly uses the same face-interpolated VoF-weighted velocity as the momentum equation. Diffusive flux assembled with VoF-averaged thermal conductivity k = αk1 + (1−α)k2. Implicit time discretisation (Euler implicit) for stability at the small Courant numbers required by the EHD interface (Co ≤ 0.5).

PIMPLE loop coupling

The enthalpy equation is solved as an outer-loop field after the pressure–velocity convergence is achieved. Temperature is back-calculated from enthalpy (T = h/cp, phase-averaged) and used to update temperature-dependent properties before the next outer iteration. This explicit property update introduces one outer-loop lag, consistent with the treatment of surface-tension in the base solver.

Temperature-dependent properties

Electrical conductivity σ(T) and relative permittivity εr(T) are updated each outer iteration using linear or Arrhenius models specified in the thermophysical properties dictionary. The updated σ and ε fields modify the charge transport equation and Maxwell stress calculation at the next electric-field solve.

Interfacial energy consistency

The enthalpy jump condition at the interface is handled through the VoF phase-fraction weighting — the same approach used for density and viscosity in interFoam. Latent heat effects (phase change) are outside the current scope; the extension targets subcooled thermal EHD without boiling.

PIMPLE Loop Structure

The modified outer-correction loop at each time step:

01
VoF advection — solve phase-fraction equation, update ρ, μ, k, σ, ε at interface
↓
02
Electric field solve — solve Laplace/Poisson equation for φ; compute E = −∇φ, fEHD, Me
↓ (PIMPLE outer loop)
03
Momentum predictor — explicit prediction with EHD body force and surface tension
04
PISO pressure–velocity — pressure equation + velocity corrector (inner loop)
05
Enthalpy transport — new: solve for h; update T, σ(T), ε(T) for next outer iteration

Taylor Flow Case

The primary test case is Taylor bubble advection through a square capillary cross-section under a transverse electric field. Taylor flow — alternating elongated bubbles and liquid slugs in confined channels — is the flow regime of interest for EHD-enhanced heat transfer in microchannels. Key physical phenomena under investigation:

  • Bubble morphology: Electric Bond number CaE = ε0εrE₀²R / σsurf governs the ratio of electric to surface-tension forces. At CaE ~ O(1), measurable bubble elongation and nose/tail deformation are expected.
  • Film thickness: The liquid film between the bubble wall and the capillary wall is sensitive to both the capillary number and the electric field — EHD-driven secondary flows can thin or thicken the film, directly affecting wall Nusselt number.
  • Velocity field redistribution: The Maxwell stress at the interface drives secondary vortices in the liquid slug. These alter the mixing and heat transfer characteristics of the slug even in the absence of phase change.
  • Courant stability: Interface cells require Co ≤ 0.5 to resolve the sharp gradient in α without numerical diffusion. The EHD electric-field solve adds no additional Courant constraint but does require iterative convergence for φ to below 10−6 at each step.

Mesh and Numerics

Capillary channel geometry meshed in OpenFOAM using blockMesh with localised refinement near the expected bubble region and walls. Interface sharpening in the VoF equation uses the counter-current compression term (compression velocity Ur). Residual convergence targets: velocity 10−5, pressure 10−4, electric potential 10−6, temperature 10−5. All spatial schemes are second-order (Gauss linear). Time integration uses Euler implicit with adaptive time-stepping controlled by the Co ≤ 0.5 constraint.

Current Status and Outcomes

  • Base enthalpy transport equation added to the solver and verified against analytical conduction benchmarks (1D transient, steady-state between isothermal walls).
  • Temperature-dependent property update loop integrated and tested for single-phase channel flow with σ(T) variation.
  • Taylor-flow EHD simulations in progress: bubble morphology data at multiple CaE values being assembled.
  • Validation against published benchmarks for EHD pumping (Taylor 1966) and Taylor bubble morphology (Bretherton 1961) with EHD forcing is ongoing.
  • Results to inform a conference or journal manuscript — target venue TBD in consultation with Prof. Absar Lakdawala.
