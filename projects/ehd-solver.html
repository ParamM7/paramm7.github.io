interEHDFoam
— energy-coupled multiphase solver.

Extending a volume-of-fluid electrohydrodynamic solver to include thermal transport — and simulating Taylor flow in capillary channels under external electric fields.

Context

Multiphase electrohydrodynamics (EHD) studies the coupling between fluid motion and externally applied electric fields across a deformable interface. The OpenFOAM solver interEHDFoam — an EHD variant of interFoam — resolves two-phase flow via the volume-of-fluid (VoF) method while incorporating a Maxwell-stress body force from a solved electric field. It is well-suited for capillary-scale problems where surface tension and electrical forces compete.

Objective

Extend the base interEHDFoam solver to couple the sensible-enthalpy transport equation — enabling thermally-driven EHD simulations in which temperature fields modulate conductivity, permittivity, and surface tension, and in turn feed back into the flow.

Methods

  • Finite-volume discretisation of the sensible-enthalpy transport equation, with diffusive and convective flux assembly consistent with the VoF phase fraction.
  • Implicit pressure–velocity coupling via PIMPLE; Maxwell-stress body force added to momentum equation.
  • Mesh-independence studies performed on capillary geometries with Courant-number control Co ≤ 0.5.
  • Iterative convergence verification for pressure, velocity, electric potential, and temperature residuals.

Taylor flow case

Simulating Taylor bubble advection through a square capillary under an external electric field. Quantifying interfacial deformation, bubble elongation, and velocity-field redistribution as a function of the electric Bond number. Studying the morphological response of the bubble nose and tail, film thickness between bubble and wall, and local Nusselt number along the bubble body.

Current status

Solver implementation in progress — base enthalpy transport added and verified against analytic conduction cases. Ongoing: validation against published benchmarks for electrohydrodynamic pumping (Taylor 1966) and Taylor bubble morphology (Bretherton 1961) with EHD forcing.

Outcomes

  • Working OpenFOAM extension reading from native dictionary entries, preserving upstream compatibility.
  • Validated thermal transport module against conduction benchmarks.
  • Body of Taylor-flow simulation results in progress — to inform a conference/journal manuscript.
