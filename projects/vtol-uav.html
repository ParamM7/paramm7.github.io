VTOL–Fixed-Wing
hybrid UAV aerodynamics.

Full-configuration CFD of a next-generation hybrid UAV: rotating MRF subdomains, transition-regime wake interaction, and induced-drag quantification — developed with Team Arrow as part of SAE International Aerodesign.

Context

VTOL–fixed-wing hybrid UAVs combine the vertical-landing envelope of a multirotor with the range of a fixed-wing aircraft. Their critical flight phase is the transition — during which propeller wakes, lifting-surface downwash, and free-stream flow interact in ways that degrade lift-to-drag ratio if not understood.

Objective

Characterise the aerodynamic behaviour of a full-configuration hybrid UAV during hover, transition, and cruise; quantify induced drag and pitching moment across the transition envelope; and propose geometry refinements to mitigate adverse wake interactions.

Methods

  • Full-configuration CAD prepared in ANSYS SpaceClaim and DesignModeler.
  • Hybrid mesh: structured prism layers in the boundary region, poly-hex core. y⁺ target ≈ 1 for k–ω SST closure.
  • Rotating Multiple Reference Frame (MRF) subdomain at ≈ 9 390 RPM for each propeller — resolving propeller-wake and lifting-surface interaction without a fully unsteady sliding-mesh solve.
  • RANS simulations in ANSYS Fluent; pressure-coefficient distributions, lift/drag polars, surface streamlines, and wake contours extracted.

Outcomes

  • Quantified induced-drag penalty and pitching-moment perturbations through the VTOL-to-fixed-wing transition.
  • Delivered simulation-driven geometry refinements that informed downstream iterations of the aerodynamic surface definition.
  • Established a reusable CFD pipeline — mesh templates, MRF zone definitions, post-processing scripts — adopted across Team Arrow's aerodynamics workflow.
